**Why this manuscript fits PLOS NTD**

Lassa fever is a WHO-prioritised neglected tropical disease that kills an estimated 5,000–10,000 people annually in West Africa, with Nigeria bearing the highest confirmed burden. Despite weekly epidemiological surveillance by the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (NCDC), no validated outbreak prediction system exists. This manuscript describes the development and retrospective validation of LassaAI — an open-source machine learning platform that predicts Lassa fever outbreak risk at the Nigerian state level 4–8 weeks in advance, achieving AUROC = 0.9994 with zero missed outbreaks on a held-out 2024–2025 test set. All code, data, and model weights are released under an MIT licence and are publicly accessible.

This work directly addresses the journal's mission to publish research that advances understanding and control of neglected tropical diseases affecting low- and middle-income countries.

**Key findings**

1. A temporally validated XGBoost model trained on 15 years of NCDC surveillance data (27,417 state-week observations) achieves near-perfect discrimination on historical data.
2. Recent case history dominates predictive signal (89% combined feature importance), with dry-season timing and meteorological features contributing modestly.
3. The model is operationalised as a free, open-source real-time forecasting dashboard (https://www.gailabai.com/lassa) and paired with a Bayesian clinical decision support copilot for healthcare workers.
4. All metrics are retrospective. Prospective validation in partnership with NCDC — the immediate next step — is the subject of a concurrent NIH Fogarty International Center R21 grant application.
